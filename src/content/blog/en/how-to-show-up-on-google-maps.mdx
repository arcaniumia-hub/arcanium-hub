When someone searches for "plumber near me" or "best tacos in town", Google shows a map with three businesses underneath it. That box is often called the **map pack**, and it is where many local customers make their choice.

This guide explains how Google decides who appears there, and the practical steps you can take to show up more often. None of it requires technical skills — just consistency.

## How Google ranks local businesses

Google says local results are based mainly on three things:

- **Relevance** — how well your business matches what the person searched for.
- **Distance** — how far your business is from the searcher, or from the location in their search.
- **Prominence** — how well-known and trusted your business is, based on reviews, links, mentions around the web and more.

You cannot move your business closer to every customer, so the work focuses on relevance and prominence. Every step below improves one or both.

## Step 1: Claim and verify your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile is the listing that appears on Maps and in the map pack. If you have not claimed it yet, go to Google Business Profile, search for your business, and follow the verification steps. Verification may involve a video, a phone call, a postcard or an email, depending on your business.

**If you serve customers at their location** — like contractors, cleaners or mobile services — set yourself up as a *service-area business*. You can hide your street address and list the cities or areas you serve instead.

## Step 2: Complete every part of your profile

A half-finished profile tells Google (and customers) very little. Fill in everything:

- **Primary category.** This is one of the most important choices you will make. Pick the category that best describes your main service, like "Roofing contractor" rather than just "Contractor".
- **Secondary categories.** Add the other services you genuinely offer.
- **Services and products.** List each service with a short description.
- **Hours.** Keep them accurate, including holiday hours.
- **Business description.** Explain what you do, who you serve and where, in plain language.
- **Website and phone number.** Link to the most relevant page on your site.
- **Photos.** Add real photos of your work, your team, your vehicles and your location. Real photos build trust far better than stock images.

## Step 3: Make your name, address and phone consistent everywhere

Your business name, address and phone number — often called **NAP** — should be identical on your website, your Google profile, Facebook, Yelp, Apple Maps, Bing and industry directories.

Small differences ("St." vs "Street", an old phone number, a previous address) make it harder for Google to be confident that all these mentions belong to the same business. Search for your business name and old phone numbers, then correct every listing you find. These listings are called **citations**, and they support your prominence.

## Step 4: Get more reviews — and reply to all of them

Reviews influence both rankings and the decision to call you. The goal is a steady flow of genuine reviews, not a burst of fifty in one week.

- **Ask at the right moment** — right after a job is finished and the customer is happy.
- **Make it easy.** Create a short review link from your Google profile and send it by text or email.
- **Reply to every review**, good or bad. Thank happy customers by name. For negative reviews, stay calm, take responsibility where it is fair, and offer to fix the problem offline.

Never buy reviews, write them yourself, or offer rewards only for positive reviews. It breaks Google's policies and can get reviews removed or your profile suspended.

## Step 5: Strengthen your website's local signals

Your Google profile and your website work as a team. Google looks at your website to understand what you do and where. Make sure:

- **Each main service has its own page**, with a clear title like "Roof Replacement in Summerville, SC".
- **Your title tags and headings** mention your service and your area naturally.
- **Your NAP appears on every page**, usually in the footer, matching your Google profile exactly.
- **You add LocalBusiness schema**, a small piece of code that tells search engines your business details.
- **Your site loads fast on phones.** Most local searches happen on mobile.

If you serve several cities, a dedicated page for each area can help — but only if each page has real, specific content. Pages that only swap the city name are low quality and rarely rank.

## Step 6: Keep your profile active

Profiles that are updated regularly tend to perform better and convert more visitors. Once a week or so:

- **Publish an update** about an offer, a recent project, a seasonal tip or an event.
- **Add new photos** of recent work.
- **Check your profile** for edits suggested by Google or the public, and correct anything inaccurate.

## Step 7: Track what matters

You cannot improve what you do not measure. Use the free tools:

- **Google Business Profile performance** shows how many people found you, called you, asked for directions or visited your website.
- **Google Search Console** shows which searches bring people to your website.
- **Google Analytics** shows what visitors do once they arrive.

Add a tracking tag (a UTM parameter) to the website link on your Google profile, so you can see exactly how many visits come from Maps.

## Mistakes that hold businesses back

- **Stuffing keywords into your business name**, like "Smith Plumbing – Best Plumber Charleston". It violates Google's guidelines and can lead to suspension.
- **Using a virtual office or P.O. box** as your address.
- **Creating duplicate profiles** for the same location.
- **Ignoring reviews**, especially negative ones.
- **Setting it and forgetting it.** Local SEO rewards steady activity over time.

## How long does it take?

Some improvements, like fixing your categories or completing your profile, can make a difference within weeks. Building prominence through reviews, citations and content usually takes **3–6 months** to show meaningful results, and keeps paying off after that. Be cautious of anyone who guarantees a top spot quickly — no one controls Google's results.
