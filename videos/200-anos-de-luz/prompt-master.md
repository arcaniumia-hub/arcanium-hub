MASTER PROMPT — "200 ANOS DE LUZ" (30-second cinematic short film)

=====================================================================
0. PROJECT SPECS
=====================================================================
- Title: "200 ANOS DE LUZ"
- Total duration: 30.000 seconds — 24 fps — 720 frames
- Format: vertical 9:16, 1080×1920 px (optional 16:9 1920×1080 variant, same timeline)
- Safe zone (Reels/TikTok/Shorts UI): keep faces and all text between y=280 px and y=1500 px
- Language: ALL spoken words and ALL on-screen text in BRAZILIAN PORTUGUESE (pt-BR), exactly as written below

=====================================================================
1. THESIS (what the film must make the viewer feel)
=====================================================================
For 200 years, every photograph was proof that light touched something real in front of a lens.
Today, an image can be born from a sentence. The film does not judge. It ends with an open question:
did photography learn to LIE, or did it learn to DREAM?

=====================================================================
2. GLOBAL DIRECTION RULES (apply to every shot)
=====================================================================
1. LIGHT IS THE PROTAGONIST. Light works (Scene 1), light witnesses (Scene 2), light becomes impossible (Scene 3), light disappears and only words remain (Scene 4).
2. THE WINDOW IS THE VISUAL MOTIF. Every scene contains a window.
3. LIGHT ALWAYS ENTERS FROM FRAME LEFT, soft and directional, like a Vermeer painting. The ONLY exception is Scene 3, where the light comes from nowhere.
4. FORBIDDEN in every shot: robots, holograms, neon, glowing circuits, digital code rain, glitch effects, sci-fi interfaces, modern LED flares, logos, brand names, watermarks, generator-rendered subtitles, distorted hands, extra fingers, warped faces, anachronistic objects.
5. Camera language evolves: Scene 1 static and patient → Scene 2 continuous push THROUGH lenses → Scene 3 slow pull-back reveal → Scene 4 locked top-down.
6. Rhythm evolves: slow → accelerating → suspended → silence.

L1 — ATTIC, BURGUNDY, FRANCE, 1826 (Scenes 1A)
Rustic attic room of a stone country house. Rough off-white lime-plaster walls, dark exposed oak beams, wide worn floorboards. A thick diagonal shaft of sunlight enters from the window with dust motes drifting slowly. On the deep stone windowsill sits a CAMERA OBSCURA: a dark walnut wooden box (about 40×25×25 cm) with dovetail joints and a brass lens tube, aimed out of the window. On a small table nearby: corked glass bottles (oil of lavender, dark bitumen), an unlit candle, a leather notebook, a few polished metal plates.

L2 — THE PLATE, MACRO (Scene 1B)
A polished pewter plate (about 20×16 cm) coated with a thin layer of dark brown bitumen, inside the dark camera box. The faint, upside-down image projected by the lens glows dimly on its surface. As the hours pass, the bitumen hardens where light hits it and pale, ghostly shapes emerge in low contrast: rooftops, the pigeon house, the tree. Texture: metallic, granular, chemical.

L3 — FIVE WINDOWS OF FIVE ERAS (Scene 2). The same composition every time: subject beside a window on frame left, light from the left, eye-level camera, medium close-up.

L4 — THE IMPOSSIBLE ROOM (Scene 3)
Quiet, timeless apartment room. Pale grey-green plaster wall. A small wooden side table with a white ceramic jug. BEHIND the woman (frame right/background) is a tall wooden window with a white frame. Outside it is DEEP NIGHT: blue-black sky, a thin crescent moon, very faint distant city lights. YET warm golden late-afternoon SUNLIGHT falls on her face, her hands and the table FROM FRAME LEFT, where there is only a plain wall with no window and no lamp. The light draws a crisp golden rectangle on the floor. Her shadow falls TOWARD the light, which is the wrong direction. Dust motes hang in the air, perfectly still, frozen.

L5 — TYPEWRITER DESK (Scene 4)
Seen from directly above (90° top-down). A vintage black manual typewriter with no visible brand, round glass-topped keys, black ink ribbon. A sheet of cream paper rolled in. Dark walnut desk. Soft daylight from frame left.
